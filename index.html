Aleksey’s Journey

  • The Early Days: At just 4 months old, Aleksey experienced sudden, severe medical emergencies. After months of hospitalizations and uncertainty, genetic testing revealed he has SCN8A, a rare genetic mutation causing dynamic and unpredictable epilepsy.
  • Overcoming Obstacles: As a resilient military family, we navigated state moves, frequent hospital visits, and constant care transitions. Through it all—and with years of therapy and specialized neurological care—Aleksey has thrived. Today, he is a bright, kind, and joyful 10-year-old who loves drawing, solving puzzles, spending time in nature, and making people laugh!
  • A New Challenge & Need for Safety: Recently, Aleksey’s seizures began occurring in his sleep. Because nighttime seizures pose significant risks without an advance warning system, he must sleep in our room for constant line-of-sight supervision.
  • Why a Service Dog? As Aleksey approaches his teenage years, we want him to experience the independence every child deserves. A professionally trained service dog through Eyes Ears Nose & Paws (EENP) will alert us to nighttime and onset seizures, provide sensory grounding for his autism, and give Aleksey the confidence to sleep safely in his own bedroom and explore the world with a faithful partner.

Understanding SCN8A & SUDEP

What is SCN8A Epilepsy?

SCN8A developmental and epileptic encephalopathy is a rare genetic mutation affecting the sodium channels in the brain. It causes unpredictable seizure activity that can change in frequency, duration, and severity throughout a child's life.

The Reality of Nighttime Seizures & SUDEP

SUDEP stands for Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy. It is a critical concern for families managing severe epilepsy, particularly when seizures occur during sleep.

Because nocturnal seizures often happen silently without mechanical warning, continuous supervision is required. A trained seizure alert service dog is capable of sensing nighttime seizure activity or onset changes, alerting caregivers immediately so prompt medical intervention can take place.
