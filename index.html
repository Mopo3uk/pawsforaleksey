Aleksey’s Journey

At 4 months old, Aleksey was the healthiest, happiest baby his doctor had ever seen. Days later, one night he suddenly stopped breathing and had his first episode, changing our lives forever. It took months of hospital stays to find the cause: SCN8A, a rare genetic epilepsy.

We’re a military family, and we’ve moved states and changed doctors again and again to find the right care. Now his seizures have started happening at night, so he sleeps in our room. Aleksey is 10, kind, funny, and endlessly empathetic.

A service dog from Eyes Ears Nose & Paws can alert us to seizures, give him sensory grounding, and give him the independence every kid deserves. Please help bring Aleksey his best friend. 🐾

Understanding SCN8A & SUDEP

What is SCN8A Epilepsy?

SCN8A developmental and epileptic encephalopathy is a rare genetic mutation affecting sodium channels in the brain. It causes unpredictable seizure activity that can change in frequency, duration, and severity throughout a child's life.

The Danger of Nighttime Seizures & SUDEP

SUDEP stands for Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy. It is a critical concern for families managing severe epilepsy, particularly when seizures occur silently during sleep.

Because nocturnal seizures often happen without mechanical warning, continuous supervision is required. A trained seizure alert service dog is capable of sensing nighttime seizure activity or onset changes, alerting caregivers immediately so prompt medical intervention can take place.
