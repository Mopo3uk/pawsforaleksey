Aleksey’s Journey

At 4 months old, Aleksey was, in his doctor's words, the healthiest and happiest baby he had ever seen. Days later, everything changed. During a bath, he went stiff, stopped breathing, and turned blue. That night our world changed forever.

The hospital couldn't find a cause. They sent us home with a diagnosis that didn't feel right. I knew my child, and I knew something was wrong. A few days later, I was back in the ER, and as we were about to be sent home, Aleksey had a seizure in my arms. That moment led us to Children's Mercy in Kansas City, 10 days in the PICU, different medications and treatment and still no answers. Not until January 2017 did genetic testing give us the name: SCN8A, a rare genetic epilepsy.

We are a military family, and Aleksey's journey has taken us from Kansas to Colorado, California, and finally Virginia. Every move meant new doctors, new schools, and starting over. After years of searching for the right neurologist and the right medication, we found a specialist who understands SCN8A. Today his seizures are less frequent, but they've started happening at night. Since then, Aleksey has slept in our room. We live with the fear of what could happen while we sleep.

And yet, through all of it, Aleksey is pure joy. He's 10 years old, kind, funny, and endlessly empathetic. He loves drawing, puzzles, the water, travel, and making new friends. We never wanted to keep him in a bubble. We want him to be a kid.

That's why we're raising funds for a service dog from Eyes Ears Nose & Paws. A trained dog can alert us to seizures, give him comfort and sensory grounding, and one day help him sleep in his own room, go out with friends, and believe in himself as he becomes a teenager.

Please help us bring Aleksey his four-legged best friend. Every donation and every share brings him closer. 🐾

Understanding SCN8A & SUDEP

What is SCN8A Epilepsy?

SCN8A developmental and epileptic encephalopathy is a rare genetic mutation affecting sodium channels in the brain. It causes unpredictable seizure activity that can change in frequency, duration, and severity throughout a child's life.

The Danger of Nighttime Seizures & SUDEP

SUDEP stands for Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy. It is a critical concern for families managing severe epilepsy, particularly when seizures occur silently during sleep.

Because nocturnal seizures often happen without mechanical warning, continuous supervision is required. A trained seizure alert service dog is capable of sensing nighttime seizure activity or onset changes, alerting caregivers immediately so prompt medical intervention can take place.
